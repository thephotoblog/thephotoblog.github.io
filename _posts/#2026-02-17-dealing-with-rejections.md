I don't have the answers. I wish I know how to *gracefully* embrace each and every of them, but I don't. And so far I have been simply riding the waves as they come and go.

I don't think I am terrible at rejections though, since it is very common in academia e.g., manuscript submission and peer-review cycle. And I have had to deal with many rejections growing up, not only in academia but also personal life. Rejections are not new to me.

However, there are just some that hurt (the ego) more than the other. Recently I have had to deal with a very huge one. It was a big disappointment. It happened during the holiday seasons too. I mean, there simply could not be a more terrible timing than this. It really broke me mentally for like a good three days. But because of the circumstances in that week, I had to quickly forget it and move on.

I think life is just like that. Sometimes things happen and can really test you. Of course, you mentally prepare yourself, plan, and even tell yourself constantly to not put all the eggs in one basket. But sometimes there are just things that are out of your control.

Even worse, sometimes the people around you don't understand a single bit of what you're experiencing (which is fine, I am not expecting them to), therefore out of a good heart (maybe?) they say shitty things like "oh, you need to learn to forget it and move on", which angered me even more.

I mean in moments like this, I truly hate the word *"need"*. I wish we can practice removing this word from our everyday vernacular. The fact that this word is used implies a lack of sympathy and holier-than-thou attitude, which I just hate.

Anyway, I can only learn from my failures, wipe away the tears and keep moving forward. Because time will not stop just because I experience a massive fail. But in the meantime, let the wave come, but don't forget to let it go.

Interestingly, through this one experience, I learn that *crying* is such an immensely cathartic process. I never knew that crying can be this helpful. Learning how to have a good cry is truly a life-saver. There are no sermons, lectures, advice that can be comparable to a one night of good, solid crying plus a bit of sobbing. Let the tears flow, and perhaps don't wipe them straightway.
